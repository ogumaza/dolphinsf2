dolphinsf2 1.6.0 converts the sound effects of Pikmin 2, on the GameCube and on the Wii. It plays the notes on the DSP's built-in oscillators. Pac-Man Vs.'s sound effects now play in the right slots. JAudio notes end where the game's volume falls silent. MusyX's linear envelopes follow the game's level more closely, and MusyX songs loop where the game loops them. dolphinsf2 converts the music and sound effects of Nintendo GameCube games to MIDI, SoundFont 2 and SFZ files. It reads games that play their sound through JAudio, and a few that play it through Factor 5's MusyX.

Requires 64-bit Windows 10 or later. No additional runtime DLLs are required. Drop a game's disc image, an extracted disc's `main.dol`, or a `.baa` or `.aaf` file onto `dolphinsf2.exe`. You can also use a terminal. `dolphinsf2 --help` lists the options. Output goes in a folder beside each input.

For macOS on Apple Silicon, follow the build instructions in the README. macOS builds include `Dolphinsf2.app`. Drop inputs onto the app in Finder to convert them.

## New in 1.6.0

- **Pikmin 2's sound effects.** Pikmin 2 plays all its sound effects in one sequence, like The Wind Waker. In The Wind Waker, that's the first sequence of the sound table. Pikmin 2's code names another sequence. dolphinsf2 used to leave Pikmin 2's effects out. It now converts each effect as that sequence plays it when the game starts the effect. dolphinsf2 recognizes the start-up archives of the USA releases on the GameCube and on the Wii. The effects come after the music. Pikmin 2's list of music now holds the sound table's first sequence, a jingle. The list used to leave the jingle out and hold the effects sequence in its place. The effects sequence played nothing as music.
- **Pac-Man Vs.'s sound effects in their slots.** The first JAudio's effects sequence gives each category of effects a number of slots. A slot is a track that waits for an effect. dolphinsf2 gave every category four slots. The Wind Waker's table gives four to each. Pac-Man Vs.'s table gives its categories other counts. The effects of two of its categories played in the wrong slots. One category's effects played the other's sounds. The other category's effects played nothing. dolphinsf2 now takes each category's count of slots from the start-up archive's table of scenes. Pac-Man Vs.'s effects now play the right sounds. The Wind Waker's files stay the same.
- **The DSP's built-in oscillators.** Programs 0xf0 and up play the DSP's built-in oscillators. dolphinsf2 used to leave their notes out and report them. The square wave, the sawtooth, the pulse of 25% duty, the triangle and the sine now become looping samples of one cycle, one for each program. Each preset of such a program has one zone, for every key and velocity. The zone holds the oscillators' envelope: full volume until the release, then a fall to silence over about 17 ms. The DSP changes its noise and its ramp as it plays. No sample can hold them. dolphinsf2 still leaves out the notes on the noise, the ramp and the other oscillators that no sample can hold. It reports them.
- **JAudio notes that fall silent.** The volume oscillator of some JAudio instruments falls to zero and holds there until the note's release. The game's voice is silent from there on. The MIDI note used to last until its release. The SoundFont's fitted decay then rang on quietly under the music. The note now ends where its volume oscillator holds at zero and no release can raise it. The zone's release then ends the sound.
- **MusyX's linear envelopes.** A MusyX envelope's linear decay or release falls in a straight line in amplitude. SoundFont and SFZ envelopes fall in decibels. dolphinsf2 used to fit a fall to silence to reach the game's level 20 dB down. That fit lay up to 5.4 dB under the game's line. The notes faded too soon. A fall to silence, or to a sustain more than 40 dB down, now holds the same energy as the game's line. It falls about 13 dB in the game's time. A note whose decay falls that far ends where the game's decay does. A note whose release takes longer than its decay ends where the fitted decay has fallen silent. A macro's fades keep the fit 20 dB down. The SoundFonts and SFZ files of songs and effects with such envelopes change. So do the MIDI files whose notes end that way.
- **MusyX's loops.** MusyX sends a section's clock back to the loop start once the section has no events left. A track that plays on past its loop entry therefore delays the loop of every track. dolphinsf2 sent the clock back at the time of the latest loop entry. The other tracks' next pass then started early. It played out of step with the track that played on. The loop now starts where the section's last event has played. The MIDI files of some songs change. Some of them get longer.
- **The README.** The list of games says that Pikmin 2 converts only in part, and why. The section "The first JAudio's sound effects" covers Pikmin 2 and the table of scenes. The section "SoundFont" describes the oscillators' zones. The section "Sequence playback" says that a note ends where its volume oscillator holds at zero. "Differences from the game" describes the new fit of MusyX's linear envelopes.
- **The libraries.** dolphinsf2 now builds on ntdlib 2.0.0, with xsflib 1.6.0 and seqlib 1.3.0.

## Known limitations

- MusyX games that the README doesn't list don't convert.
- Pikmin 2 converts only in part. Many of its pieces of music come out with no notes. Their root tracks run an interrupt routine in every tick. Some of those routines poll the game once a beat and never return. dolphinsf2 stops such a track and reports that it runs without waiting. Many of Pikmin 2's sound effects play quieter than in the game.
- dolphinsf2 converts the sound effects of the USA releases of The Wind Waker, The Wind Waker HD, Pac-Man Vs. and Pikmin 2. Other releases of those games convert without their effects. So do the other games that use the first JAudio.
- dolphinsf2 plays each sound effect of the first JAudio in the first slot of its category. The game's code passes values with some effects, such as the material of the ground under a footstep. A conversion passes none unless `--port` gives them. Some effects then play nothing. A game keeps some effects going by starting them again and again. dolphinsf2 plays each effect from one start: to its end, or with loop markers where it loops.
- A MusyX effect's files play it as MusyX does when the game starts it and leaves it alone. The game's code can change an effect's controllers while it plays, or stop it. A macro can sweep a sample's pitch, move its pan, or change its key or volume while the sample plays. dolphinsf2 leaves those changes out. It reports the sweeps and the moving pans. A macro can also take its reverb send from a controller or a variable. dolphinsf2 leaves that send out and reports it.
- Each note of a MusyX effect plays on a MIDI channel of its own. The notes leave channel 10 free for drums. An effect therefore plays at most 15 notes. A note whose voices sound on, such as a looping sample, stops 10 seconds after the effect's last change.
- The fitted release of a MusyX linear envelope goes on falling after the game's release has ended.
- Notes on the DSP's automatic mixer play at half the level of the others. That level approximates the game's mix.
- The rest are as in 1.5.0, apart from the changes above. See "Differences from the game" in the README for the full list.
