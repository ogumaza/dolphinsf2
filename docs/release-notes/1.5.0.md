dolphinsf2 1.5.0 converts the sound effects of the seven MusyX games that it reads. A macro's Panning now moves the voices of MusyX songs too. Nintendo Puzzle Collection's songs now play their samples at the rates that their macros set. Their SFZ files now play the right samples. dolphinsf2 converts the music and sound effects of Nintendo GameCube games to MIDI, SoundFont 2 and SFZ files. It reads games that play their sound through JAudio, and a few that play it through Factor 5's MusyX.

Requires 64-bit Windows 10 or later. No additional runtime DLLs are required. Drop a game's disc image, an extracted disc's `main.dol`, or a `.baa` or `.aaf` file onto `dolphinsf2.exe`. You can also use a terminal. `dolphinsf2 --help` lists the options. Output goes in a folder beside each input.

For macOS on Apple Silicon, follow the build instructions in the README. macOS builds include `Dolphinsf2.app`. Drop inputs onto the app in Finder to convert them.

## New in 1.5.0

- **The sound effects of the MusyX games.** dolphinsf2 now converts the sound effects of Mario Golf: Toadstool Tour, Mario Power Tennis, Mario Party 4, Pokémon Colosseum, WarioWare, Inc.: Mega Party Game$!, Nintendo Puzzle Collection and the NDDEMO disc. The effects come after each game's songs. dolphinsf2 lists the effects that each game's code starts. It plays each one as the game starts it. Each effect takes the volume and the pan that the game gives it. An effect's MIDI file plays every voice that MusyX starts for it. That includes the voices that the effect's macros start. The voices follow the volumes, pans and sends that the macros set. WarioWare's songs that the game plays as effects convert with the effects. Each effect gets a name such as `se-0036` in Mario Party 4 or `mario-0154` in Mario Golf. The README's new section "MusyX's sound effects" describes the effects of each game.
- **Panning in MusyX songs.** A MusyX macro can set its voice's pan with its Panning step. The voices of songs used to leave that pan out. The conversion reported the step. A song's voice now takes the pan. Three songs change: Mario Party 4's Play with Bowser, and Nintendo Puzzle Collection's dr_mario-fever and dr_mario-feverf. In dr_mario-fever and dr_mario-feverf, two keys of track 9 used to play in the centre. They now play hard right and hard left. Only the three songs' SoundFonts and SFZ files change.
- **MusyX's SetPitch.** A MusyX macro's SetPitch step sets the rate at which its voice plays its sample. dolphinsf2 used to leave the step out and report it. The voice then played at its note's pitch. A voice now plays its sample at the rate that SetPitch sets, whatever its key. Only Nintendo Puzzle Collection's macros use the step. The songs of Dr. Mario and Panel de Pon change, with some of the menu's songs. Their SoundFonts and SFZ files change. So do some of their MIDI files, in which a sample's end ends a note.
- **Nintendo Puzzle Collection's SFZ samples.** The SFZ files' shared folder named each MusyX sample by its ID alone: `s27.wav`, for example. Nintendo Puzzle Collection's groups give many IDs more than one sample. A song of another group then played the wrong sample in its SFZ files. The SoundFonts played the right samples. A later group can give an ID another sample. That sample's name now holds the group's number too: `s7_27.wav`, for example. Only Puzzle Collection's SFZ files change.
- **Reports.** A conversion's report no longer says "SoundFont attenuated by 0.0 dB" for a cut too small to show. The Wind Waker's reports held such lines. The files stay the same.
- **The README.** The section "Differences from the game" gains an item on sound effects. The section "SFZ files" says how the shared folder names a MusyX sample.
- **The libraries.** dolphinsf2 now builds on ntdlib 1.13.0, with xsflib 1.6.0 and seqlib 1.3.0.

## Known limitations

- MusyX games that the README doesn't list don't convert.
- A MusyX effect's files play it as MusyX does when the game starts it and leaves it alone. The game's code can change an effect's controllers while it plays, or stop it. A macro can sweep a sample's pitch, move its pan, or change its key or volume while the sample plays. dolphinsf2 leaves those changes out. It reports the sweeps and the moving pans. A macro can also take its reverb send from a controller or a variable. dolphinsf2 leaves that send out and reports it.
- Each note of a MusyX effect plays on a MIDI channel of its own. The notes leave channel 10 free for drums. An effect therefore plays at most 15 notes. A note whose voices sound on, such as a looping sample, stops 10 seconds after the effect's last change.
- dolphinsf2 converts the sound effects of the USA releases of The Wind Waker, The Wind Waker HD and Pac-Man Vs. Other releases of those games convert without their effects. So do the other games that use the first JAudio. Pikmin 2 is one of them: it starts its effects another way.
- dolphinsf2 plays each of The Wind Waker's sound effects in the first slot of its category. The game's code passes values with some effects, such as the material of the ground under a footstep. A conversion passes none unless `--port` gives them. Some effects then play nothing. A game keeps some effects going by starting them again and again. dolphinsf2 plays each effect from one start: to its end, or with loop markers where it loops.
- Notes on the DSP's automatic mixer play at half the level of the others. That level approximates the game's mix.
- The rest are as in 1.3.1, apart from the changes above. See "Differences from the game" in the README for the full list.
